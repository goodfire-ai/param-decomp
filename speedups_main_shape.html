Speedups for the main-branch shape

Assumes the parallel-layerwise-runs macro-strategy is likely off the table and the loss shape stays roughly what main runs today: stochastic subset recon + importance minimality + faithfulness, with PPGD's role open (kept, shrunk, or replaced by more/better stochastic samples). Re-derived from a fresh read of main, not from the prior docs.  ·  2026-06-10  ·  supersedes ppgd_speedups_strategy_robust.html where they conflict.

TL;DR. A fresh cost model of a main-branch training step (llama-l18 config) says the step is ~450 bf16-TFLOP-equivalents per rank, split roughly: PPGD ~55%, stochastic-subset recon ~18%, frozen-prefix recompute hidden inside every masked forward ~16%, faithfulness/weight-delta matmuls in fp32 ~9%, CI fn ~3%. Four findings change the picture from the previous docs:

(1) Backwards are already suffix-only — autograd stops at the decomposed layer because nothing upstream requires grad — but every masked forward still pays the full prefix. For the single-layer configs in flight, prefix caching (already written on the branch) applies to main today and removes ~16% of the step with zero quality risk. (2) PPGD's before_backward duplicates an entire backward that total_loss.backward() already performs — the source tensors are leaves; their grads are deposited for free. ~10% of the step for a ~10-line change. (3) The fp32 weight-delta materialization + faithfulness backward is a sleeping giant: ~9% on the one-layer config and it scales with total component params, so on any full-model config it becomes the dominant term. A factored delta-application plus a sketched (Hutchinson) faithfulness loss removes every weight-shaped matmul from the step. (4) The hot path forces ~18 GPU→CPU syncs per step (loss .item()s and tensor asserts) at a per-rank batch of 4×512 — classic launch-starvation territory; cadence-gate or _assert_async them.

Algorithmically, the biggest single lever is unchanged if PPGD stays — kill warmup (n_warmup 2→0 via vertex warm-start + optimism, ~35% of the step) — and if PPGD goes, the natural replacement is already in the house style: tilted (CVaR) weighting over the stochastic subset samples you already pay for, which buys adversarial pressure with zero extra forwards at n=1 (per-position tilting) and modest cost at n>1. After the per-step wins stack (~2.5–3×), the remaining multiplier on everything is step count (100k–200k): a Muon-class optimizer for the matrix-shaped U/V is the highest-upside unexplored axis.

1 · The shape being optimized

Reference: the live llama-l18 config (param_decomp_lab/experiments/lm/llama-3.1-8b-l18.yaml) — Llama-3.1-8B, three matrices of layer 18 decomposed at C=24576 each, batch 128 / dp 32 (per-rank 4×512 tokens), losses = ImportanceMinimality + StochasticReconSubset (uniform-k, 1 sample) + PersistentPGDRecon (n_warmup=2, per-batch-per-position) + Faithfulness (coeff 1e5), use_delta_component: true, bf16 autocast. One step of Trainer.run (param_decomp/optimize.py:524-670) does:

1. calc_weight_deltas()             — U@V materialized per module, fp32, outside autocast
2. target forward (cache inputs)    — full 8B forward, no graph (frozen)
3. CI fn forward                    — 1.2B-param shared transformer + heads
4. metric updates:
     StochasticReconSubset          — 1 masked forward
     PPGD warmup ×2                 — masked forward + backward-to-sources, each
     PPGD final                     — 1 masked forward
     Faithfulness                   — ‖Δ‖² on the materialized deltas
5. before_backward (PPGD)           — autograd.grad to sources, retain_graph=True
6. total_loss.backward()            — components + CI fn (+ sources again, unused)
7. optimizer steps, source step

Two structural facts that the rest of this doc leans on:

Backwards are suffix-only; forwards are not. Every grad-requiring leaf (component masks, CI values, sources, weight deltas) lives at the decomposed sites or later. Autograd therefore never traverses the frozen prefix below layer 18 — each backward costs ≈ 2× the suffix, not 2× the model. But every masked forward recomputes the prefix from scratch, and it's identical to the target forward's prefix every time. On the l18 config that is 18/32 of an 8B forward, four times per step, pure waste.

Weight-shaped work scales with total components, not with the batch. calc_weight_deltas + the faithfulness backward cost O(C·d_in·d_out) per module per step, replicated identically on all 32 ranks, in fp32 by design (optimize.py:548). On a one-layer config it's ~9% of the step; decompose all 32 layers and it becomes the single biggest term. Any plan that ends at full-model decomposition has to fix this line item.

Per rank, per step, llama-l18 config, in bf16-TFLOP-equivalents (fp32 matmuls counted ~2×). Static estimates from shapes — to be checked against a profile (E7) before anything expensive is built on them.

Component overhead per masked forward is real but secondary here: C(d_in+d_out)/(d_in·d_out) ≈ 7.7× the original site cost, but one MLP layer is small against an 8B suffix. It becomes primary in full-model configs — same scaling warning as faithfulness.

Beyond FLOPs, two non-compute taxes: (a) each recon loss materializes a 2048×128k logits tensor and runs log_softmax over it (param_decomp_lab/batch_and_loss_fns.py:61-70) — memory-bandwidth-heavy and a peak-memory spike, 4–5× per step; (b) ~18 forced GPU→CPU syncs per step (see E1). DDP all-reduces ~10GB of fp32 grads (1.36B component + 1.2B CI-fn params) per step — overlapped and probably not binding now, but it becomes binding once compute drops 2–3×.

3 · Engineering improvements NO ALGORITHM CHANGE

Ordered by payoff/effort. E1–E4 are quality-neutral by construction.

E1 · Prefix caching for masked forwards (apply the branch's residual cache to main, today)

SAVES: ~16% of step (more once PPGD shrinks) · EFFORT: port from branch · RISK: none (bit-exact)

All decomposed sites sit in one layer, so the residual stream entering that layer is identical across the target forward and all 4 masked forwards. Cache it once per batch from the target forward; start every masked forward there. use_cached_residual on feature/multipool already implements this — the previous doc filed it under "keyed to layerwise runs", but it applies to the current main-branch single-layer configs unchanged. The only thing to add is a guard: assert all decomposed sites share a layer (or take the earliest), so multi-layer configs fall back to full forwards rather than silently breaking. Note backwards gain nothing (already suffix-only) — this is a forward-side win, which is exactly why it stacks with E2 rather than overlapping it.

E2 · Delete the duplicated PPGD source-grad backward

SAVES: ~10% of step · EFFORT: ~10 lines · RISK: none (exact rescale)

PPGD sources are leaf tensors with requires_grad=True (persistent_pgd_state.py:256). total_loss.backward() already deposits coeff · ∂L/∂source into source.grad. Yet before_backward runs a whole separate autograd.grad(..., retain_graph=True) over the same subgraph (persistent_pgd_recon.py:243-246) — a full extra suffix backward per step, plus the memory cost of retaining the graph. Fix: drop before_backward's grad pass; in after_backward, read source.grad, divide by the coeff, zero it, do the scope's all-reduce there (per-batch-per-position skips it anyway), then step. Identical update, one backward fewer.

E3 · Kill the weight-shaped matmuls: factored delta + sketched faithfulness

SAVES: ~9% now, dominant share on full-model configs · EFFORT: days · RISK: low (delta part exact) / E-X5 (sketch part)

Three sub-moves, escalating: (a) The fp32-outside-autocast materialization exists for FaithfulnessLoss precision; the delta-component path doesn't need fp32 — split precisions (bf16/TF32 materialization for the masked-forward path) for a free ~2× on this term. (b) Apply the delta in factored form inside masked forwards: Δx = Wx − U(Vx) — token-shaped instead of weight-shaped, and its backward to U,V is rank-(tokens) instead of two dense C·d_in·d_out matmuls. At 2048 tokens/rank and C=24.6k this is cheaper even before counting the backward. (c) Replace the exact faithfulness loss with a Hutchinson sketch: ‖Δ‖²_F = E‖Δv‖² with k probe vectors, each probe costing one Wv + one U(Vv) — k=16 probes is ~500× cheaper than materialization, unbiased, with autograd giving unbiased grads. Faithfulness has a 1e5 coefficient and a quadratic landscape, so gradient noise here is plausibly harmless — but that's an experiment (X5), and (a)+(b) are worth it alone. Keep the exact ‖Δ‖² for eval/logging at eval cadence. Together (b)+(c) make per-step cost independent of weight shapes — the thing that currently rules out cheap full-model runs.

E4 · De-sync the hot path

SAVES: unknown until profiled — historically large at this per-rank batch · EFFORT: ~day · RISK: none

Per step: .item() on every metric loss + total (optimize.py:578-588), torch.isfinite(...).all() asserts on the 2048×128k logits and every CI tensor (optimize.py:171-191), per-metric isfinite asserts (optimize.py:572), and two range-asserts per module inside calc_causal_importances itself (component_model.py:349,353). Each forces a device sync; ~18/step at per-rank batch 4×512 means the GPU repeatedly drains while Python waits. Keep the fail-fast culture without the stalls: torch._assert_async for the tensor asserts (dies on the GPU, no sync), and accumulate loss scalars as tensors, .item() only on should_log_train steps. The branch's profiling history (a day lost to one misattributed 600ms wait) says measure before/after, not trust.

E5 · Fused/chunked linear-KL on the vocab head

SAVES: memory spike + bandwidth per recon fwd; enables larger per-rank batch · EFFORT: port from branch · RISK: none

param_decomp/fused_linear_kl.py on the branch (or Liger-style chunking) computes the KL without materializing full logits. FLOP-wise the unembed is only ~5% of a masked forward, so the win is memory and bandwidth — but the freed peak memory is what lets per-rank batch grow above 4, which attacks the launch-overhead problem (E6) from the other side. Port it; it passes every robustness test.

E6 · Batch the sample axis; compile/graph the masked forward

SAVES: launch overhead + utilization at batch 4 · EFFORT: days · RISK: low

The step runs 5+ separate full-model forwards at per-rank batch 4 through a Python-hook-based forward (component_model.py:231-300) — small kernels, per-module Python in the middle, prime launch-bound territory. Two moves: fold n_mask_samples / PPGD n_samples draws into the batch dim (one forward at batch 4k instead of k at batch 4 — the mask plumbing already supports arbitrary leading dims), and torch.compile (or CUDA-graph) the component-site region. Matters more, not less, if the future shape leans on more stochastic samples (A1).

E7 · Port the profiling toolchain first; re-profile before building

EFFORT: port from branch · RISK: none

The per-phase cpu/gpu/wait tracer + sync-debug env var + standalone reproducers from the branch (_trace.py, scripts/standalone_repros/). Every number in §2 is a static estimate; the split between FLOP-bound, bandwidth-bound, and launch-bound at per-rank batch 4 is exactly the thing static estimates get wrong. This gates which of E1–E6 to do first, and it's reusable across every future shape change.

E8 · Distributed hygiene (becomes binding after the compute wins land)

SAVES: comm halving + ~16GB/rank memory · EFFORT: days · RISK: low

~10GB fp32 grads all-reduced per step: register DDP's bf16 compression hook (2×); consider PowerSGD specifically for component grads later (speculative — U/V grads may compress well, but validate against decomposition quality). ZeroRedundancyOptimizer shards the ~21GB of replicated AdamW state — that's memory, which is batch size, which is E6's problem solved a second way. Also minor: the eval loop's unconditional empty_cache()+gc.collect() every eval pass (optimize.py:647-648), and the O(steps) loader replay on resume.

4 · Algorithmic improvements CHANGES WHAT'S COMPUTED

A1 · Adversarial pressure from the samples you already pay for (tilted stochastic subsets)

RELEVANT IF: PPGD shrinks or goes · COST: ~zero at n=1, linear-and-parallel at n>1 · RISK: X4

If the future shape is "some kind of stochastic subset recon losses", the cheapest adversary is a reweighting of those losses, not a separate search. Two rungs: (i) per-position tilting, free — within the existing single sample, weight the per-position KL by softmax(KL_pos/τ) instead of the mean. The worst positions are where masks actually break the model; the mean buries them. Zero extra forwards, one config knob, and it reuses the per-position losses the recon already computes before reduction. (ii) per-sample CVaR — n parallel subset draws (batched along E6's sample axis), loss = tilted mean over samples. This is a stochastic-search adversary: no state, no warmup, no serial depth, trivially DDP-compatible, and it composes with the subset-routing distribution rather than fighting it. The open question is whether soft-max-weighted random search matches persistent gradient search at equal compute — that's X4, and it's the experiment most aligned with where the team's discussions already are.

A2 · If PPGD stays: n_warmup 2→0 via vertex warm-start + optimistic step

RELEVANT IF: PPGD stays · SAVES: ~35% of the current step · RISK: X3 (catastrophic-overfitting checks)

Carried over from the previous doc, still the largest single lever in the PPGD-stays world, and E2 makes it cheaper to implement: the final backward's source grads are now free, so initialize the next step's effective sources at the vertex the grads point to (the loss is locally linear in the masks — the vertex is where 2 PGD steps were crawling), plus an optimistic offset along the Adam momentum already sitting in AdamPGDOptimizer._m. The stochastic-recon term at equal coefficient is the standard guard against the degenerate-adversary failure mode, and it's already in the loss.

A3 · Choose the subset family for cacheability (full-model configs)

RELEVANT IF: multi-layer / full-model decomposition · SAVES: ~half of every masked forward · RISK: changes the subset distribution — quality check needed

E1's prefix cache works because all masked sites share a layer. For full-model configs, uniform-k routing scatter-masks everywhere and no prefix is clean. But the subset family is a modelling choice: route by layer cut-point (sample ℓ, mask only sites ≥ ℓ, possibly subset within the suffix) and every masked forward reuses the target forward's prefix up to ℓ — average saving ~half the forward, and the residuals at candidate cut points are already in memory from the cached target forward. Whether suffix-shaped subsets train as good a decomposition as uniform-k is a real question (the prefix sites get masked less often unless ℓ's distribution compensates — e.g. weight toward early ℓ), but it's a cheap ablation (X7) and it's the only way masked-forward cost stops scaling with model depth in a full-model regime.

A4 · Variance reduction so fewer samples do the work of more

COST: free · RISK: low · APPLIES: any stochastic shape

n_mask_samples is 1 because samples are expensive; make each one count more. Antithetic pairs (draw r and 1−r with shared routing — negatively correlated losses, lower-variance gradient at 2 samples than 2 independent draws), and common random numbers across adjacent steps for the routing masks (smoother loss surface step-to-step, kinder to Adam). Both are a few lines in calc_stochastic_component_mask_info. Worth bundling into X4's sweep rather than testing alone.

A5 · Step-count levers: Muon for U/V, and the untouched scaling axes

UPSIDE: multiplies everything (100k–200k steps) · RISK: three-player dynamics · COST: cheap to pilot small

Every per-step win above caps out around 2.5–3×; the step count is a bigger number. The component parameters are exactly the shape Muon-class optimizers are built for (large 2D matrices, currently AdamW + aggressive clip at 0.01), and nobody has swept the optimizer/lr/batch axes for this objective since the loss shape stabilized. Risks are real — the components/CI-fn/adversary system is a game, not a loss, and faster component movement stresses whatever adversary tracking exists (interacts with A2/X3) — so pilot on TMS/resid-mlp/gpt2-scale first (X6). Also in this bucket: SVD-based component init (start near-faithful, possibly replacing the 400-step faithfulness warmup and improving conditioning) — cheap to try, modest expected value.

A6 · CI-fn diet

SAVES: ~3-7% compute now, ~4.4GB/step comm, 300M head params · RISK: CI quality · COST: config-level

The global-shared-transformer CI fn is ~1.2B params for a 3-site decomposition, ~25% of it the d_model→ΣC output head. A low-rank head (d_model→r→ΣC, r≈512) cuts head params ~8× with the obvious quality check; trunk depth (4 blocks) is another knob. Secondary as compute, but it's a quarter of the DDP gradient traffic and it scales with ΣC — same full-model scaling warning as everything else. (The amortized-adversary idea from the previous doc — sources predicted by a CI-fn-shaped head — remains the long-game version of this if PPGD stays; unchanged, still gated on evidence that converged sources are predictable.)
