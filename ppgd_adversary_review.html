The PPGD adversary under multipool

Review of the feature/multipool draft, proposals for a better scheme, and which of the earlier speed-up ideas survive a distributed setup.  ·  Dan / PD team  ·  2026-06-09

TL;DR. The multipool draft is good engineering, but it has one structural property that no amount of overlap engineering will fix: the three pools run in lockstep, so step time = max(pool time) + sync overhead, and the adversary pool is the only pool whose cost does not shard. The chunkwise pool scales out by adding chunks; the CI pool scales by data parallelism; the PPGD pool must run full-model forwards on a full V/U replica with full-model CI shipped in — its per-rank cost is fixed by model size, and its inner loop is irreducibly serial. At full-model scale the CI→PPGD edge also becomes the largest wire payload in the system.

An empirical fact constrains the design space: layerwise PPGD was tried and underperforms full-model PPGD (unlike the stochastic loss, where layerwise ≥ subset). Since the chunkwise pool's stochastic loss is already layerwise, PPGD is the only term in the multipool objective that exercises joint all-sites masking — and the negative result says that joint coverage is exactly where its value lives. So the joint forward and joint loss are load-bearing and must be preserved; what can be changed is when the adversary's gradient lands, and how its search is performed.

My main recommendations, in order: (1) break the lockstep — let the adversary contribute one-step-stale gradients so it leaves the critical path entirely while its loss stays exactly the joint full-model objective (cheap, ~portal-level change, gating experiment is ~20 lines in the single-pool trainer); (2) shard or cheapen the adversary's search without decomposing its loss — update-sharded block-coordinate refinement, layerwise proposals selected under the joint loss, the single-pass greedy cascade (revived by the negative result); (3) longer-term, an amortized adversary head on the CI pool trained against the joint loss, for which the multipool architecture is accidentally pre-adapted. Most of my earlier ideas survive; the ones that get stronger under a distributed scheme are exactly the ones that convert serial inner-loop depth into parallel width.

1 · Glossary

Sources / masks / warmup (project terms)
Sources r ∈ [0,1] are the raw per-component values PPGD optimizes adversarially; the forward consumes masks m = g + (1−g)·r where g is the causal importance (CI) value. Warmup = the n_warmup_steps inner gradient-ascent updates on the sources before the final loss; the final fused backward supplies one more source update (n_warmup+1 total per step).
Site / chunk / pool / portal (multipool terms)
A site is one decomposed weight matrix. A chunk is a group of sites owned by a set of chunkwise ranks. A pool is one of the three rank groups (CI fn / chunkwise V-U / PPGD adversary). A portal is the branch's typed object for one cross-pool communication edge.
Adjoint
The gradient of the loss with respect to an intermediate activation — here, ∂L/∂(output of a decomposed module). Backprop computes adjoints layer by layer on its way to parameter gradients. If you cache a module's adjoint and its input, the gradient of the loss w.r.t. that module's masks is a cheap local product — no new backward needed (because the module output is linear in its masks: out = Σ_c m_c (a·V_c) U_c). The cached adjoint is only approximate once weights or other layers' masks move ("stale adjoint").
Box constraint / vertex
The sources live in a box: each coordinate independently in [0,1]. A vertex is a corner (every coordinate 0 or 1). The maximum of a linear function over a box sits at a vertex and can be written down directly from gradient signs — no iterative search.
Amortized optimization
Replacing a per-instance inner optimization with a learned function that predicts the solution in one forward pass. The search cost is paid once, during training of the predictor, and "amortized" over all future instances. A semi-amortized scheme predicts a warm start, then takes one or two refinement steps.
Tilted / CVaR weighting
Given k sampled losses, instead of the mean, weight samples by softmax(loss/τ) — a soft maximum. As τ→0 you recover the worst sample; at moderate τ you optimize the average of the worst tail (CVaR, "conditional value at risk"). Turns "find the worst case by sequential search" into "sample candidates in parallel and up-weight the bad ones".
Optimistic / extragradient step
In min-max optimization, updating one player against a prediction of the other player's next position (e.g. extrapolating along its momentum) rather than its current position. Provably stabilizes and accelerates adversarial dynamics; the practical payoff here is that an extrapolated source can substitute for an explicit warmup step.
Staleness / lockstep
A gradient is stale if it was computed against parameters from an earlier step. Pools run in lockstep when every pool must finish step T before any pool starts T+1; an asynchronous design lets a pool's contribution arrive a step late (bounded staleness) so the slow pool stops pacing everyone.
Residual-start / prefix cache
Branch trick (use_cached_residual): when all masked sites live in one block, cache the clean residual stream entering that block once per batch, and run only the block + suffix for every masked forward. A "prefix cache" is the same idea stated generally: layers before the first masked site compute exactly the target model, so their activations can be reused from the target forward.

Worth stating explicitly because it changes my earlier rankings — the branch already implements several items from my previous lists: the fused multi-target backward (one autograd.grad over V/U + CI + sources in step_ppgd.py — the old "two backwards on the final forward" is gone); residual-start prefix caching for single-block runs; a fused linear-KL kernel; compile_ppgd plumbing; and broadcast-scope sources as a memory answer. The remaining serial core — n_warmup sequential full-model forward+backwards plus the final recon — is what's left, and it is the part none of the engineering so far has touched.

3 · Structural critique

Per your instruction I'm not trusting the profiling numbers on the branch. Everything below is an argument from structure; §6 says what to re-measure with the branch's own toolchain before acting.

3.1 · The adversary is the only pool that doesn't shard

Chunkwise cost per rank falls as you add chunks (fewer owned sites each). CI cost per rank falls as you add CI ranks (smaller batch slice each). PPGD cost per rank is constant in pool size for the serial dimension: every PPGD rank runs n_warmup+1 full-model forwards and ~n_warmup+1 backwards regardless of how many PPGD ranks exist — extra ranks only shrink its batch slice, and the warmup steps cannot be spread across ranks because step k+1 depends on step k. A distributed scheme buys parallel width; the PPGD inner loop is serial depth. By construction, as you scale the other pools out, PPGD becomes the pole — so "PPGD is still the bottleneck" is plausible a priori whatever the current traces say (the 2026-06-03 calibration had LW 1244 ms ≳ PPGD 1140 ms at big512, i.e. they were already nearly tied at that topology — and chunkwise has the easy knob of more chunks while PPGD has none).

3.2 · Lockstep synchrony puts the adversary on the critical path twice

The per-step cycle is: PPGD computes g_VU → ships to chunkwise → chunkwise combines, steps AdamW → ships updated V/U → PPGD blocks on the recv before its next step. So chunkwise can't finish step T without PPGD's slowest phase (warmup + recon + backward + reduce), and PPGD can't start T+1 without chunkwise's tail. The design doc's own calibration attributed ~47% of the step to "overhead" — the price of keeping three heterogeneous pools in sync. The async-recv refinements in the DESIGN.md backlog shave milliseconds off this; they don't change the shape.

3.3 · The full-model CI edge does not scale to many blocks

The PPGD pool needs CI values for all sites on its batch slice: a B_ppgd × T × ΣC tensor each step, plus the same-shaped g_CI going back. For a single block this is manageable. For a full model (ΣC in the hundreds of thousands per token), this edge becomes tens of GB per step in each direction — it is already the edge that deadlocked seq-2048 fan-out runs, which is why the 2-pool merged it away. Any scheme that keeps a separate full-model adversary pool keeps this payload (2-pool dodges the wire but still pays the memory co-located).

3.4 · Per-position persistent sources poison checkpointing and resume

The branch already collided with this: sources are the only data-shaped (not parameter-shaped) state, ~2.3 TB at batch 1280, can't be consolidated, get sharded per rank into ppgd_<S>/rank_<r>.pth, and tie a PPGD resume to the exact pool layout. And under streaming data the persistence is largely fictitious anyway — at step T+1, source slot (b,t) refers to a different token of a different document, so what the warmup is really doing is repairing a stale init every step. The state you actually want to persist is whatever generalizes across steps: a shared per-position source (the broadcast scope — real persistence but a much blunter adversary), a low-rank/structured source, or an adversary function (amortization). The current per-position default gets the worst of both: maximal memory, minimal genuine persistence.

The lens for everything that follows: a distributed setup can buy width (more ranks, more parallel forwards, more sites at once) but never depth (sequential inner steps). So under multipool, ideas that convert the adversary's serial depth into parallel width — sampling, amortization, closed-form vertex steps, staleness — gain value; ideas that merely make each serial step cheaper (truncated KL, fp8, compile) remain useful but bounded; and ideas that add depth are dead on arrival.

4 · Better schemes

4.0 · The constraint: what the layerwise-PPGD negative result tells us

Layerwise PPGD — restricting the adversarial loss to one site per forward — was tested and underperforms full-model PPGD, even though the same restriction helps the stochastic loss. The natural reading: the stochastic loss's job (training CI and components for local ablatability) decomposes across sites because random-mask errors are roughly independent per layer; the adversary's job is precisely the part that doesn't decompose — finding compounding failure modes, where small per-site divergences that random sampling never flags amplify or conspire when all sites are masked simultaneously. A layerwise adversary is structurally blind to compounding. And in the multipool objective the layerwise stochastic loss means PPGD is the only joint-masking term — it carries all of the "the decomposition works when everything is masked at once" signal.

Design consequence: the joint forward and joint loss are fixed points; the degrees of freedom are the gradient's timing (staleness), the search procedure (how sources are found), and the source parameterization (how they're stored). Schemes are evaluated against that constraint below. There may still be relaxations between layerwise and full-model that work — they're collected in Scheme 2′ — but the prior on aggressive loss decompositions is now low.

Four proposals, ordered by (implementation cost × risk). They compose: 1 is a stepping stone to 3; 2′ supplies the cheap search kernels that 1 and 3 schedule.

Scheme 1 — Break the lockstep: a one-step-stale adversary

Change: PPGD stops participating in step T's gradient. It computes g_VU and g_CI against the V/U it already holds (from step T−1's ship) and sends them off; chunkwise and CI apply whatever adversary gradients have arrived — i.e. the adversary's contribution to step T is computed against step T−1's parameters. The blocking updated_vu_from_chunk … wait() at the end of step_ppgd and the g_VU wait in step_chunkwise both become genuinely asynchronous: each pool runs at its own natural rate, and step time collapses to max(chunkwise, CI) plus much less synchronization.

Why it's sound: the adversarial recon term is an auxiliary loss at coefficient ~0.5, not the primary objective; one step of staleness on an auxiliary gradient is the mildest form of the bounded-staleness regime that asynchronous SGD and pipeline-parallel training (PipeDream's weight stashing) have used for years. Better: the PPGD term's own premise is that the adversary persists across steps because parameters drift slowly — the same assumption that justifies persistent sources justifies a one-step-stale gradient. The optimistic-update trick (extrapolate sources along their Adam momentum) directly compensates the staleness if needed.

What it buys beyond speed: once PPGD is off the critical path, its now-slack wall-clock can be spent on quality — more warmup steps, more samples, bigger effective adversary — at zero step-time cost. The bottleneck question dissolves rather than being optimized.

Cost / risk: low. The portals already exist; this is a re-plumbing of when sends are consumed, plus removing two waits. The convergence question is testable in the single-pool trainer first (§6, E1). Main risk: a subtle interaction where the stale adversary chronically lags a fast-moving phase of training (early steps); mitigate by starting PPGD synchronous and going async after start_frac — the config field already exists.

Scheme 2′ — Keep the joint loss; shard and cheapen the search

Status: the raw version of this scheme — a layerwise adversarial loss folded into the chunkwise pool — is dead: it was tested and underperforms (§4.0). What survives is a family of relaxations that all share one invariant: the loss that trains components remains the full-model joint adversarial recon; only the procedure that finds the sources is decomposed, approximated, or parallelized. Ordered from most to least conservative:

  • (a) Update-sharded block-coordinate refinement. The warmup forward masks all sites (joint, unchanged), but each PPGD rank refines only its assigned subset of layers' sources per step, with the rest held at their persisted values; assignments rotate. This is block-coordinate ascent on the unchanged joint problem: the inner search parallelizes across ranks (n ranks ⇒ n coordinate blocks refined per step instead of 1), while every gradient the components see is computed under joint masking. Unlike layerwise PPGD, compounding is fully visible to the loss — the only approximation is that each rank's refinement holds other layers' sources fixed within a step, which is also what sequential PGD effectively does between its few steps.
  • (b) Layerwise proposals, joint selection. Use cheap per-site machinery (closed-form vertex from cached mask-gradients, per-site PGD against a cached adjoint) only to generate candidate source configurations; assemble candidates into joint masks and score them under the full-model loss with tilted weighting. The per-site step is a proposal distribution, not an objective — the negative result doesn't apply to it, because nothing layerwise ever reaches the components' gradient.
  • (c) Single-pass greedy cascade (F3, revived). One joint forward in which each masked module, in depth order, sets its sources to the locally-worst vertex using its live input (which already reflects the upstream attacks) and a one-step-stale adjoint. This constructs a coordinated cross-layer attack in forward order — compounding is exactly what it captures, and it captures it in a single forward with zero source state. The layerwise-PPGD failure is evidence for this idea, not against it: it says coordination matters, and the cascade is the cheapest coordinated search available.
  • (d) Suffix-cumulative curriculum. Mask sites l..L jointly with the prefix clean (so prefix activations cache from the target forward), sampling or annealing l. Compounding within every suffix is exercised; l=0 recovers the full joint loss. This does change the loss (it's a relaxation, not a reparameterization), so it carries the same scientific risk class as layerwise PPGD — test it only if (a)–(c) leave compute on the table, and anchor it with periodic l=0 passes.

Cost / risk: (a) is plumbing on the existing pool (the warmup's get_grads already takes a source list — restrict it per rank and rotate); (b) and (c) are new but small search kernels; (d) is an experiment. Risk is graded exactly by how far each item is from the joint loss: (a)–(c) leave it untouched, (d) relaxes it.

Scheme 3 — Amortized adversary head on the CI pool

Change: replace persistent sources + warmup with a small trained head A_φ that predicts adversarial sources from the same per-component inner activations the CI fn already consumes (or from the shared CI trunk, for global_shared_transformer). It lives on the CI pool, runs in the same forward as the CI fn, and its predicted sources ride the existing CI-values portals as extra channels. Trained as the maximizing player: the recon pools already extract ∂loss/∂sources in their fused backward — flip the sign, ship it back along the existing g_CI portal (same shape, same routing), and the CI pool steps A_φ with its own optimizer.

Why the architecture is accidentally pre-adapted: every hard part of an amortized adversary in a distributed setting — where does it live, how do its outputs reach the consumers, how do its gradients come back, how is it checkpointed — is answered by machinery the branch already built for the CI fn, because the CI fn is already an amortized predictor of per-component per-position values trained from recon-pool gradients. The adversary head is its mirror twin with the objective negated. Concretely solved for free: parameter-shaped checkpointing (the TB source shards and the layout-coupled resume of §3.4 vanish); no warmup on anyone's critical path (n_warmup=0; optionally one vertex refinement at the consumer); real persistence (the adversary's knowledge accumulates in φ, which generalizes across tokens, batches, and steps — fixing the warm-garbage problem of streaming per-position sources).

The negative result sharpens two design choices here. First, A_φ must be trained against the joint loss — which the plumbing above already gives for free, since the source-gradients shipped back come from the recon pool's joint fused backward; nothing layerwise enters. Second, it raises the expressivity bar: a purely per-component local head (each component's source from its own inner activation alone) may be unable to represent the cross-layer coordination that evidently matters, so the shared-trunk variant (a head on the global_shared_transformer CI trunk, which sees all sites' activations) is the right starting point, not the optional upgrade.

Cost / risk: highest of the three — this changes training dynamics (a GAN-like min-max between A_φ and the components), and min-max training can be finicky; the optimism/extragradient toolkit and a "predict-then-one-vertex-refine" semi-amortized fallback are the stabilizers. I'd sequence it after Scheme 1 (async makes the adversary's training loop free to run continuously against stale weights — the classic stabilizer for the slow-learner player) and gate it on the E3 diagnostic showing converged sources are predictable from CI/activations.

Scheme 4 — Minimal change: dead-time warmup (if you keep the current shape)

Change: keep the synchronous design, but move warmup out of the critical path into the PPGD pool's dead time. The CI pool already prefetches the next batch's target activations in its dead time (phase A4); extend that to compute and ship CIT+1 during step T's tail. The PPGD pool then runs step T+1's warmup while it's otherwise blocked waiting for updated V/U (phase E) — against one-step-stale V/U, which the final (fresh-V/U) recon + fused backward corrects. The only adversary work remaining on the critical path is the single final forward + the fused backward it already shares.

Cost / risk: low-medium; it's the same staleness bet as Scheme 1 but confined to warmup (the gradient that trains components stays fresh), so it's strictly more conservative. Buys less: the final full-model forward+backward and the CI edge remain, so §3.1/§3.3 still bind eventually. Reasonable as the first shipped increment if E1's staleness result looks at all worrying.
